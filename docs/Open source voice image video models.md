# One key covers most of AIMVA's media

No single model generates speech, transcribes audio, makes images and makes video in November 2026, but single *providers* do. fal.ai, which AIMVA already uses, covers image editing, video, avatar video, ElevenLabs TTS and Whisper STT under one key. Google's Gemini API is the only first-party provider that offers TTS with GA Hindi, Tamil, Telugu and Marathi, STT through audio input, Nano Banana image editing and Veo video under one key. The gap in both is the real-time Indian-language voice agent. There, Sarvam (Bulbul TTS at about ₹1 per 1,000 characters, STT at ₹0.49/min) clearly leads on quality and price in the only benchmark found, which a vendor ran. On the open-source side, the safest commercial picks are Kokoro, Chatterbox and AI4Bharat's IndicF5 for voice, Whisper and IndicConformer for STT, Qwen-Image-Edit-2511, Z-Image and FLUX.2 [klein] 4B for images (all Apache 2.0 or MIT), and Wan 2.2, Wan2.2-S2V and InfiniteTalk for video and avatars. FLUX 3 Image Klein (under $5M revenue) and LTX-2.3 (under $10M revenue) are usable while AIMVA stays small. Several top-ranked "open" models are traps: Qwen-Image-2.1, Fibo 2.0, FLUX.2 [dev], XTTS-v2, F5-TTS weights, Wav2Lip, and the geo-fenced HunyuanVideo and MiniMax H3. The cheapest good paid options are Nano Banana 2.1 or FLUX 3 Klein for images ($0.009–0.034), Veo 3.1 Lite or Pruna P-Video for video ($0.02–0.08/s with audio), and Sarvam or Gemini Flash TTS for voice. The recommendation: keep fal as the single backend key for image, video, avatars, English voiceovers and captions. Add Sarvam as one deliberate second key for Indian-language voice, meaning the voice agent and Indic voiceovers. Put everything behind a capability-level interface so any model can be swapped with a config change, because the Sora 2 API shutdown on Sept 24, 2026 showed how fast a provider can drop a model.

## Licences, not leaderboards, set the open-source shortlist

The most important finding for a commercial SaaS is that "open weights" and "commercially usable" have drifted apart in 2026. The top two models on Artificial Analysis's open-weights image board, **Qwen-Image-2.1 (Elo 1045) and Bria Fibo 2.0 (1039), cannot be used commercially**. Qwen-Image-2.1 ships under the Qwen Research License, "FOR NON-COMMERCIAL PURPOSES ONLY" ([mixed-news](https://mixed-news.com/en/qwen-image-2-1-transparent-rgba-7b-open-weights-research-licence/)). Fibo 2.0 has "gated weights for non-commercial use only" ([grainzilla](https://grainzilla.com/brias-fibo-2-0-edits-images-with-four-references-and-open-weights-but-commercial-work-needs-a-license/); [AA leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/image/leaderboard/text-to-image/open-weights)). The same split shows up in voice and video, so the shortlist below is filtered by licence first and quality second.

### Voice: permissive models exist, but few speak Telugu or Tamil

Per-licence checks find **Kokoro-82M (Apache 2.0) and Resemble's Chatterbox family (MIT) to be the strongest permissive English TTS options**. Chatterbox Multilingual covers 23 languages with zero-shot cloning, and Kokoro runs on CPU ([Yaps.ai](https://www.yaps.ai/blog/best-local-tts-models)). Chatterbox-Turbo claims sub-200 ms inference, which suits telephony, though that is the vendor's figure ([Dograh](https://www.dograh.com/feeds/blog/open-source-tts-comparison)). The licences to watch are XTTS-v2, which has decent Hindi but uses the non-commercial Coqui Public Model License, and F5-TTS, whose code is MIT but whose weights are CC BY-NC ([Yaps.ai](https://www.yaps.ai/blog/best-local-tts-models)). For Indian languages the permissive option is AI4Bharat. **IndicF5 is MIT-licensed and covers 11 Indian languages including Tamil and Telugu**, trained on 1,417 hours of speech ([AIKosh IndicF5](https://aikosh.indiaai.gov.in/home/models/details/aibharat_indicf5.html)). Indic Parler-TTS is listed as MIT ([AIKosh](https://aikosh.indiaai.gov.in/home/models/details/ai4bharat_indic_parler_tts_text_to_speech_model.html)), but its Hugging Face card needs checking. For STT, Whisper large-v3 remains the permissive multilingual default, and hosted turbo costs about $0.04/hour of audio ([IA Decider](https://iadecider.com/articles/speech-to-text-reference-2026)). NVIDIA's documentation names no Indic coverage for its newest Parakeet and Canary models ([NVIDIA NeMo](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo/speech/latest/asr/featured_models.html)). That leaves AI4Bharat's IndicConformer as the Indic specialist, though its licence and per-language WER were not verified.

### Image: Apache-licensed Qwen and Z-Image anchor a self-host stack

The commercial-safe image core is **Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 for instruction editing and Qwen-Image or Z-Image Turbo for generation, all Apache 2.0** ([invideo Qwen](https://invideo.io/blog/qwen-image-ai-generator/); [invideo Z-Image](https://invideo.io/blog/z-image-ai-image-generator/)). The licence status comes from a secondary source, so confirm it on each model card. **FLUX 3 Image Klein** is the best-ranked open model AIMVA can use today (Elo 1043, close behind Qwen-Image-2.1). It is free for commercial use below **$5M annual revenue**, runs on 8 GB of VRAM, and handles editing and multi-reference generation ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/black-forest-labs-opens-flux-3-image-klein-weights-with-free-commercial-use-limited-to-smaller-businesses)). Self-hosting FLUX.2 [dev] needs BFL's "Platform" licence, which is priced only through sales. The "Builder" tier is explicitly "not meant for client use or downstream applications", so it does not fit a SaaS ([BFL licensing](https://bfl.ai/licensing)). Stability's Community License covers SD 3.5 only below $1M revenue ([Stability](https://stability.ai/license)).

### Video and avatars: Apache 2.0 Wan is commercially clean but more than a year old

**Wan 2.1 and 2.2 are Apache 2.0, but every later Wan release (2.5 through 3.0) is closed and API-only** ([Atlas Cloud](https://www.atlascloud.ai/blog/tips/is-wan-3.0-open-source)). The strongest open-weight model on the leaderboard is MiniMax H3 (Elo 1160). Its licence excludes the **US, EU, UK and South Korea** and caps revenue at $20M ([NYU Shanghai RITS](https://rits.shanghai.nyu.edu/ai/minimax-ships-h3-weights-with-the-us-and-eu-excluded/)). HunyuanVideo 1.5's licence "DOES NOT APPLY" in the EU, UK and South Korea, and it requires AI-generated content to be labelled conspicuously ([HF LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/tencent/HunyuanVideo-1.5/blob/main/LICENSE)). AIMVA serves Indian customers, so these territorial carve-outs bite less than they would for a Western SaaS, but they still block any future EU expansion. LTX-2.3 is the practical middle ground: native synchronized audio, 16 GB FP8 inference, and royalty-free use below **$10M revenue**. Its use restrictions bar impersonation without consent, deepfakes included ([LTX-2.3 LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/Lightricks/LTX-2.3/blob/main/LICENSE)). For presenters, **Wan2.2-S2V-14B (Apache 2.0, image plus audio to talking video, but at least 80 GB of VRAM)** ([HF](https://huggingface.co/Wan-AI/Wan2.2-S2V-14B)) and **InfiniteTalk (Apache 2.0, unlimited duration)** ([HF](https://huggingface.co/MeiGen-AI/InfiniteTalk)) are the clean options. Wav2Lip is research-only, and SadTalker's Hugging Face bundle carries Wav2Lip's non-commercial checkpoint ([talking-avatar.dev](https://talking-avatar.dev/alternatives/10-best-open-source-lip-sync-alternatives-2026)).

Hosted APIs partly dissolve the licence question. Replicate states that FLUX.1 [dev] images generated on Replicate "can be used commercially", but not images made from downloaded weights ([Replicate](https://replicate.com/black-forest-labs/flux-dev)). fal marks each model with a "Commercial use" or "Research only" badge ([fal FAQ](https://fal.ai/docs/documentation/model-apis/faq.md)). AIMVA's rule should be to call only badge-cleared endpoints and log the model ID with every output.

## Self-hosting rarely beats the cheapest APIs in 2026

Paid APIs have fallen so far that self-hosting is mostly a licence-control or data-residency decision, not a cost decision. On voice, Sarvam's ₹1 per 1,000 characters and Groq's $0.04/hour of Whisper STT are already near compute cost. The only self-host data point is a vendor estimate of about ₹0.10–0.30 per minute for AI4Bharat TTS on AWS Mumbai ([Caller Digital](https://caller.digital/blog/indic-tts-benchmark-bulbul-elevenlabs-sarvam-google-ai4bharat-2026)). On images, rented H100 or A100 time works out to roughly $0.01–0.025 per SDXL-class image ([imagebench](https://imagebench.pages.dev/learn/cost-speed)), no better than FLUX 3 Image Klein's **$0.009 per 1K image** on BFL's own API ([BFL pricing](https://bfl.ai/pricing)). Video is starker. Wan 2.1 14B takes about **10–12 minutes per 5-second 720p clip on an H100, roughly $0.42–0.50 on demand** ([Spheron](https://www.spheron.network/blog/deploy-wan-2-1-ai-video-generation-gpu-setup/)). Veo 3.1 Lite or Pruna P-Video produce the same clip *with audio* for $0.10–0.40. Self-hosting pays off only for small, distilled models used for drafts, such as Wan 1.3B at about $0.03–0.04 per clip, or for brand-specific LoRAs on Apache-licensed Qwen or Z-Image. The steelman for self-hosting is real: provider churn and fal's opaque per-model terms are risks. Still, the more efficient hedge at AIMVA's stage is a swappable API layer rather than a GPU fleet.

## Cheapest good paid models: Sarvam, Nano Banana, Veo Lite

### Voice: Sarvam leads Indian languages at about a third of ElevenLabs' price

The only per-language benchmark found was run by a vendor that routes traffic across all four providers, and its raw data is available only on request. Treat it as directional. In it, **Sarvam Bulbul scored MOS 4.5 in Hindi, 4.4 in Tamil and 4.3 in Telugu, against ElevenLabs' 4.2, 3.8 and 3.7**. It also pronounced 86–92% of Indian names correctly, against ElevenLabs' 58–78%, with a 180 ms first-audio latency ([Caller Digital](https://caller.digital/blog/indic-tts-benchmark-bulbul-elevenlabs-sarvam-google-ai4bharat-2026)). AI4Bharat's open model beat both ElevenLabs and Google on Tamil and Telugu. Sarvam's November 2026 price list puts **Bulbul v4 Flash at ₹1.00 per 1,000 characters, Bulbul v3 at ₹3.00, and STT at ₹0.49 per minute**, excluding GST ([Sarvam pricing](https://docs.sarvam.ai/api/getting-started/pricing)). That works out to roughly $11–12 per million characters for v4 Flash. Globally, the cheapest high-Elo TTS is **Speechify Simba 3.2 at $6.58 per million characters** (Elo 1239). Google's Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS (Elo 1265) costs $16.49, Cartesia $49, and ElevenLabs Eleven v4 (Elo 1319, top rank) $80 ([Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/best-text-to-speech-models-september-2026-ranked-priced)). These Elo rankings are mostly English, so they say little about Telugu. Google's Gemini TTS is GA for hi-IN, mr-IN, ta-IN, te-IN and en-IN, with Gujarati, Kannada, Malayalam, Odia and Punjabi in preview ([Google Cloud](https://docs.cloud.google.com/text-to-speech/docs/gemini-tts)). Its 2.5 Flash TTS works out to about **$0.015 per minute of speech** ([Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)), which makes it a credible single-key fallback. For a 30-second ad voiceover, TTS costs less than ₹1 to about $0.05 on any of these, so voiceovers should be chosen on quality alone.

For the 60-second lead-callback agent, platform fees stack on top of the models. **Bolna charges $0.06/min with STT, LLM and TTS included**, Vapi charges $0.05/min for hosting only, and ElevenLabs Agents charges $0.08/min ([VoiceAgentPrices](https://voiceagentprices.com/)). Among speech-to-speech models, **Gemini 3.8 Live costs about $0.02/min against about $0.30/min for OpenAI gpt-realtime-2.1** ([Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/openai-vs-google-vs-qwen-voice-ai-apis-2026/)), but its quality in Telugu and Tamil is unverified. A self-assembled stack (Sarvam STT, Bulbul v4 Flash, a cheap LLM, and an open framework such as LiveKit or Pipecat) should land around ₹2–3 per minute before telephony, by my estimate. Indian bundled vendors charge ₹3.5–6.8/min plus ₹649/month per +91 number ([Outpero](https://outpero.com/blog/vapi-vs-bolna-vs-retell-for-india)). Whichever stack AIMVA picks, it still needs a +91 line, DLT registration and DND screening, and Vapi, Bolna and Retell document no DLT handling.

### Image: Nano Banana 2.1 is the price-quality sweet spot

The top of the image leaderboards is closed and expensive. **GPT Image 2.5/2.6 at max quality leads both text-to-image and editing at about $0.21 per image** ([AA T2I](https://artificialanalysis.ai/image/leaderboard/text-to-image); [AA editing](https://artificialanalysis.ai/image/leaderboard/editing)). **Nano Banana 2.1 ranks #4 in text-to-image and #7 in editing at $0.0336 per 1K image, and half that in batch** ([BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/media-pricing/nano-banana)). That makes it the default for scene and background edits. For drafts and variations, FLUX 3 Image Klein ($0.009) and FLUX.2 [klein] 4B (about $0.005/MP on fal) are an order of magnitude cheaper ([BFL pricing](https://bfl.ai/pricing); [teamday](https://www.teamday.ai/blog/ai-api-pricing-comparison-2026)). Text rendering is where AIMVA's Hindi needs bite. GPT Image 2 is the only model documented rendering Hindi and Bengali, while Ideogram 4 wins English posters but is weak on non-Latin scripts ([invideo](https://invideo.io/blog/best-ai-model-text-in-images/)). No benchmark of Devanagari rendering exists, so AIMVA should render ad copy as real fonts in a layout layer and use generators only for imagery. The same caution applies to product fidelity. Every editor regenerates pixels and can alter labels, so the robust pipeline is to segment the product, generate or outpaint the scene (FLUX Erase at $0.03, Outpainting at $0.045), and composite the original product pixels back on top. Midjourney still has no official API and is unusable for a backend ([Apiframe](https://apiframe.ai/blog/best-midjourney-apis)).

### Video: native audio now costs $0.02–0.08 per second

**OpenAI shut down the Sora 2 API on Sept 24, 2026, and Google retired its Veo 3.1 preview IDs on Oct 22, 2026** in favour of `gemini-omni-1.1-flash` ([aifreeapi](https://aifreeapi.com/en/posts/sora-2-vs-veo-3-affordable)). The cheapest current options with native audio are **Pruna P-Video at $0.02/s (720p) and $0.04/s (1080p), and Veo 3.1 Lite at $0.05/s and $0.08/s**. Grok Imagine Video costs $0.05–0.08/s and LTX-2.3 Fast $0.06/s ([invideo pricing](https://invideo.io/blog/ai-video-model-pricing/)). On fal, Wan 2.7 costs $0.10–0.15/s and Veo 3.1 with audio $0.40/s ([fal](https://fal.ai/learn/tools/best-text-to-video-apis-2026)). OpenRouter lists Wan 3.0, the #1 model on Artificial Analysis's text-to-video board, at **$0.0425/s** ([OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/collections/video-models)). On quality per dollar, **HeyGen Video 1 (Elo 1153, $2.40/min) and MiniMax H3 (Elo 1160, $4.80/min)** sit within about 20 Elo of Wan 3.0 (1172, $12/min) ([AA T2V](https://artificialanalysis.ai/video/leaderboard/text-to-video)). Image-to-video rankings, which matter most for animating product photos, were not captured. For avatars, **HeyGen Avatar V costs $0.10/s on fal**, or $6/min. Tavus at $0.80–1.00/min and D-ID at about $1/min are far cheaper per finished minute ([fal](https://fal.ai/learn/tools/best-text-to-video-apis-2026); [Fora Soft](https://www.forasoft.com/blog/article/heygen-alternatives)). A 15-second 1080p reel therefore costs about $0.60 on Pruna, $1.20 on Veo Lite, or $1.50 as a HeyGen presenter.

## Google is the only first-party one-key option, and fal is the practical one

The single-*model* dream is not here yet. Google's Gemini Omni was billed as "create anything from any input", but its first model outputs **video only, with image and audio output promised later** ([BusinessWorld](https://www.bworldonline.com/technology/2026/05/20/751019/google-launches-gemini-omni-capable-of-chat-based-video-generation-editing/)). Qwen-Omni models do speech in and speech out but generate no images or video ([Alibaba Model Studio](https://alibabacloud.com/help/en/model-studio/models)). Among single *providers*, **Google's Gemini API is the only first-party one covering all four needs under one key**: Gemini TTS with GA Indic languages, STT through audio input (no standalone STT product), Nano Banana images from $0.039, Veo 3.1 Lite/Fast/Standard video at $0.05–0.40/s, and Live speech-to-speech ([Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). Its gaps are voice cloning, which is still in preview, and no confirmed avatar or lip-sync API. OpenAI dropped out of the one-key race when it killed video. Alibaba Model Studio offers Wan 3.0, Qwen-Image 3.0 and Qwen TTS/ASR under one key, but its Indian-language support is unverified.

For AIMVA, **fal is the better primary key because it is already integrated and covers more of the specific jobs**. It offers image editing (Nano Banana, FLUX, Qwen, Seedream), video (Veo, Kling, Wan, Seedance, LTX, PixVerse), HeyGen Avatar V, ElevenLabs voices, and Whisper v3 STT at $0.07/hour ([Aireiter](https://aireiter.com/blog/fal-ai-review-2026); [IA Decider](https://iadecider.com/articles/speech-to-text-reference-2026)). The trade-offs are concrete. New accounts start at **2 concurrent requests, rising automatically to 40** with spend. Media is kept for at least 7 days by default. Accounts lock when credits fall below a threshold unless the customer is on invoice billing ([fal FAQ](https://fal.ai/docs/documentation/model-apis/faq.md)). Markups also vary. Veo costs $0.40/s everywhere, but Kling v3 Pro is $0.112/s on fal against $0.224 on Replicate, and Seedream 5.0 Pro is $0.0675 on fal against $0.045 direct from BytePlus ([TeamDay](https://teamday.pages.dev/blog/ai-api-pricing-comparison-2026)). What neither fal nor Google provides is Sarvam-grade Indic voice or a telephony-ready agent runtime. So "one key" is achievable for ad creative (image, video and voiceover) but not for the voice agent without a quality sacrifice. OpenRouter is emerging as a third route that covers LLMs plus video, with HeyGen avatars at a promotional $0.01/s, but its image and voice depth was not verified.

## Recommended stack and model-swap pattern for AIMVA

The stack below keeps **fal as the single backend key for image, video, avatars, English voiceovers and captions**. It adds **Sarvam as the one deliberate exception, for Indian-language voice: the real-time agent and Indic voiceovers**. Google's Gemini API is held as a warm fallback key, so one aggregator outage or delisting cannot take a capability offline.

| Capability | Primary (fal unless noted) | Fallback | Open-source escape hatch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product scene / background edit | Nano Banana 2.1 + segment-and-composite of original product | FLUX 3 Image Klein; Google direct Nano Banana | Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 (Apache 2.0) |
| Drafts / variations | FLUX 3 Image Klein or FLUX.2 [klein] 4B (~$0.005–0.009) | Z-Image Turbo | Z-Image Turbo, FLUX.2 [klein] 4B |
| Ad copy text, esp. Hindi | Real fonts in an HTML/SVG layout layer | GPT Image 2.x medium where text must be generated | — |
| Object removal / outpaint | FLUX Erase / Outpainting | Nano Banana 2.1 | Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 |
| Product-to-video reels | Veo 3.1 Lite ($0.05–0.08/s, audio) | Pruna P-Video; MiniMax H3; Wan 3.0 via OpenRouter | Wan 2.2 TI2V-5B, LTX-2.3 (under $10M revenue) |
| Presenter / avatar | HeyGen Avatar V ($0.10/s), consented or synthetic faces only | Tavus or D-ID (~$1/min) | InfiniteTalk, Wan2.2-S2V (Apache 2.0) |
| Ad voiceover | ElevenLabs via fal (English); Sarvam Bulbul v3 (Indic) | Gemini 2.5/3.x Flash TTS | IndicF5, Chatterbox, Kokoro |
| Transcription (captions) | Whisper v3 on fal ($0.07/h) | Gemini audio input | Whisper large-v3, IndicConformer |
| Real-time voice agent | Sarvam STT + Bulbul v4 Flash, on LiveKit/Pipecat or Bolna, plus a +91 DLT-registered line | Gemini Live (test Indic quality first) | IndicF5 + IndicConformer self-hosted at volume |

The swap pattern matters more than any single pick, because model churn now happens monthly. AIMVA's backend should expose **capabilities, not models**: `image_edit`, `image_generate`, `image_to_video`, `text_to_video`, `avatar_video`, `tts`, `stt` and `voice_agent`. Each has one internal request/response schema. A config-driven registry, held in a database table or YAML rather than in code, maps each capability and tier ("draft", "standard", "premium") to an ordered list of routes. Each route records the provider, a **pinned** model ID or version (fal's parameters change without warning), a price per unit, the licence badge, supported languages, and a timeout. A thin adapter per provider translates the internal schema to that provider's API. Because most routes go through fal, most adapters reduce to "fal endpoint plus parameter mapping", and adding a model is a registry row plus a small mapping, not a new integration. The router walks the list in order, falling back on errors, timeouts or moderation refusals. It submits jobs asynchronously with idempotent job IDs and webhooks, copies every output into AIMVA's own storage at once, before fal's retention window closes, and writes the model ID, licence badge, cost and prompt with each asset for audit and per-customer billing. A small golden set of AIMVA test prompts, including Hindi, Tamil and Telugu voice lines, Devanagari layouts and real SMB product photos, should run against any candidate model before its registry row goes live. With that in place, swapping Nano Banana for the next GPT Image, or Veo Lite for the next Omni Flash, is a config change and a re-run of the evaluation.

## Conclusion

The practical answer to "one key for everything" is that AIMVA already has it for creative work. fal plus a swappable registry covers images, video, avatars, English voiceovers and captions. The only thing worth a second key is Indian-language voice, the agent above all, where Sarvam's quality and price lead is large and telephony compliance has to be solved outside any media API anyway. The bigger strategic shift is that licences, not benchmarks, now decide what is "open". The best open weights are increasingly research-only or geo-fenced, and the clean permissive models (Wan 2.2, Qwen-Image-Edit-2511, IndicF5) trail the frontier by a year. Open source is therefore AIMVA's insurance policy and future cost lever, not its day-one engine.

Three questions remain open and are worth testing directly before committing spend. First, whether Gemini Live and Gemini TTS hold up in Telugu and Tamil phone calls, which would let Google replace Sarvam and cut a key. Second, which image-to-video model best animates real product photos, since that leaderboard was not captured. Third, whether any generator reliably renders Devanagari ad copy. The revenue caps on FLUX 3 Klein ($5M) and LTX-2.3 ($10M) also need a calendar reminder, because they become paid licences as AIMVA grows.
